Privacy

Last updated October 15, 2026

This is a personal career site. It has no accounts, sign-ups or forms. It measures visits with Google Analytics, and the services that run the site see some technical details of each visit.

Google Analytics

When a page loads, a script from Google records the visit and sends it to Google. It also records when you click a link that leaves this site, such as the link to LinkedIn.

  • What is recorded: the page's address and title, the page you came from, the link you clicked to leave and where it points, the date and time, your browser, device type, operating system, screen size and language, and your approximate location (country, region and city), which Google works out from your IP address.
  • Cookies: Analytics stores two cookies, _ga and _ga_XWCRC21WXW. They hold a random ID so that a return visit counts as the same visitor. They don't contain your name or email address. They last up to two years unless you delete them.
  • Why: to see how many people visit, which pages and links get attention, and where visitors come from.
  • Who receives it: Google. Its servers may be in the United States or other countries. Google says Analytics doesn't store IP addresses. I see reports by page, link, location and device. They aren't tied to your name or email address.
  • How long it's kept: To confirm: the event data retention setting in GA4 (2 or 14 months).

Hosting and server logs

This site runs on Railway. Like most web servers, it writes a log line for each request, with your IP address, the time, the page you asked for and whether it loaded.

  • Why: to keep the site running and to find and fix problems.
  • Who receives it: Railway, and me.
  • How long it's kept: To confirm: log retention on the Railway plan in use.

Fonts

The site's fonts load from Google Fonts. Your browser fetches them from Google's servers, so Google receives your IP address, your browser details and the page that asked for them. Google Fonts doesn't set cookies.

Your choices

This site doesn't show a cookie banner or ask for consent. Analytics starts as soon as a page loads. The site doesn't respond to Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signals.

What you can do:

  • Block Analytics. Many content blockers, and the tracking protection in some browsers, stop Google Analytics from loading. Google also offers an opt-out browser add-on for desktop browsers. The site works normally either way.
  • Delete or block the cookies. Deleting _ga and _ga_XWCRC21WXW removes the ID. If you allow cookies again, a later visit gets a new one.
  • Block Google Fonts. A content blocker can stop the fonts from loading. The site then uses your device's own fonts.
